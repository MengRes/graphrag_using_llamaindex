# MedGraph AI: Healthcare Knowledge Graph Platform Case Study

## Chapter 1: Background and Clinical Motivation

MedGraph AI is a healthcare technology initiative launched in 2022 by the Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory in collaboration with Mayo Clinic and Kaiser Permanente. The project addresses a critical challenge in modern medicine: clinicians and researchers struggle to synthesize knowledge scattered across millions of clinical notes, research papers, drug databases, and treatment guidelines.

Traditional electronic health record (EHR) search returns individual documents but cannot answer questions like "What treatment pathways have been most effective for drug-resistant pneumonia in immunocompromised patients over the past decade?" MedGraph AI applies GraphRAG methodology to medical literature and de-identified clinical records to enable such global clinical queries.

Dr. Catherine O'Malley, principal investigator at Johns Hopkins, leads the MedGraph AI research consortium. Dr. Raj Patel from Mayo Clinic directs the clinical validation program, which involves twelve hospital systems across the United States. The project received $18.5 million in funding from the National Institutes of Health (NIH) under grant R01-LM013847.

## Chapter 2: Data Sources and Ingestion Pipeline

MedGraph AI ingests four categories of medical data, each requiring specialized preprocessing.

**PubMed Central Open Access** provides approximately 4.2 million biomedical research articles. The ingestion pipeline downloads XML full-text articles, extracts title, abstract, body sections, and bibliographic metadata, then stores them as LlamaIndex Document objects. As of January 2025, 890,000 articles have been fully indexed.

**Clinical Practice Guidelines** from the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN), American Heart Association (AHA), and Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) are parsed from PDF format using a custom layout-aware parser developed by the Hopkins team. These guidelines contribute structured treatment recommendation entities to the knowledge graph.

**FDA Drug Labels** (Structured Product Labels) are downloaded from DailyMed and parsed into drug entity nodes with properties including active ingredient, indication, contraindication, and dosage form. MedGraph AI maintains relationships between drugs and the conditions they treat, creating a medication knowledge subgraph.

**De-identified Clinical Notes** from three participating hospital systems (Mayo Clinic, Kaiser Permanente Northern California, and Johns Hopkins Hospital) are processed under strict HIPAA compliance. Notes are de-identified using the NIH-scrubber tool before ingestion. Only 50,000 notes were included in the initial pilot due to governance review timelines.

## Chapter 3: Medical Entity Extraction and Ontology Mapping

Medical entity extraction presents unique challenges compared to general-domain GraphRAG. MedGraph AI uses a specialized extraction prompt tuned for biomedical terminology and maps extracted entities to standard ontologies.

The extraction prompt instructs the LLM to identify entity types including Disease, Drug, Gene, Protein, Procedure, Symptom, AnatomicalStructure, and Organization. Each entity is annotated with a Unified Medical Language System (UMLS) Concept Unique Identifier (CUI) through post-extraction mapping against the UMLS Metathesaurus.

Relationship types follow the Predication Argument Schema used in SemMedDB, including treats, causes, inhibits, activates, diagnoses, and co-occurs_with. The extraction model is GPT-4o fine-tuned on 12,000 annotated biomedical sentences from the BioCreative V corpus.

Evaluation on a held-out set of 500 clinical abstracts showed entity extraction F1 of 0.87 and relation extraction F1 of 0.72. Dr. Patel's team identified that relation extraction accuracy drops significantly for relations spanning more than one sentence, motivating the use of larger chunk sizes (2048 tokens) for clinical text.

MedGraph AI logs every extraction event with the source document ID, chunk offset, extracted entities with UMLS CUIs, and extracted relations with confidence scores. This audit trail is required for clinical governance review.

## Chapter 4: Clinical Community Detection and Use Cases

Community detection on the MedGraph AI knowledge graph reveals clinically meaningful clusters that align with disease domains and treatment pathways.

Applying hierarchical Leiden with max_cluster_size=8 produced 2,340 communities at the leaf level. Notable communities include a cardiovascular cluster (containing entities such as atorvastatin, myocardial infarction, and percutaneous coronary intervention), an oncology cluster (pembrolizumab, non-small cell lung cancer, PD-L1), and an infectious disease cluster (methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus, vancomycin, sepsis protocol).

Community summaries generated by GPT-4o are reviewed by clinical domain experts before deployment. Dr. O'Malley's team developed a review interface where cardiologists, oncologists, and infectious disease specialists rate summary accuracy on a five-point scale. The average expert rating across 200 reviewed summaries was 4.1 out of 5.

Three clinical use cases have been validated in pilot deployments. **Treatment Pathway Discovery** allows oncologists to query optimal sequencing of therapies for specific cancer subtypes. **Drug Interaction Analysis** traverses the medication subgraph to identify potential adverse interactions across a patient's prescription list. **Literature Synthesis** enables researchers to ask global questions about emerging trends in a disease area without manually reviewing hundreds of papers.

## Chapter 5: Regulatory Compliance and Future Directions

MedGraph AI operates under a complex regulatory framework governing medical data and clinical decision support tools.

All patient data processing complies with HIPAA Privacy Rule and the HIPAA Security Rule. The de-identification pipeline is validated annually by an independent auditor. PubMed and FDA data are public domain and unrestricted. Clinical practice guidelines are used under licensing agreements with NCCN and AHA.

The FDA regulates clinical decision support software under the 21st Century Cures Act. MedGraph AI is currently classified as a Class II medical device software requiring 510(k) premarket notification. Dr. Patel leads the regulatory submission team, targeting FDA clearance by Q3 2026.

Future research directions include multimodal graph construction from radiology images and pathology slides, real-time graph updates as new clinical notes are authored, and federated GraphRAG across institutions without centralizing sensitive patient data. Johns Hopkins has submitted a follow-on NIH grant proposal for $25 million to extend MedGraph AI to pediatric medicine and rare disease research.

The MedGraph AI consortium plans to open-source its medical extraction prompts and evaluation benchmarks at the AMIA 2025 Annual Symposium, contributing to the broader medical AI research community.
